## 📄 Abstract
Dose-dense adjuvant chemotherapy prolongs survival in breast cancer and has been translated into neoadjuvant settings. Although weekly nanoparticle albumin-bound (nab)-paclitaxel yields superior pathological complete response (pCR) rates relative to solvent-based paclitaxel, data supporting its incorporation into a dose-dense neoadjuvant regimen-particularly for hormone receptor positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 negative (HR+/HER2-) tumours-remain limited. This trial therefore evaluated whether a bi-weekly dose-dense nab-paclitaxel schedule improves the proportion of residual cancer burden (RCB) 0/Ⅰ compared with a comparable dose-dense docetaxel regimen, each followed by dose-dense epirubicin plus cyclophosphamide.
This was a multicentre, randomised, phase III trial done at three hospitals in China. HR⁺/HER2⁻ patients (cT1-3/N0-1/M0) were randomised 1:1 using dynamic allocation with Pocock minimisation on Ki-67 and TNM stage. The experimental arm received nab-paclitaxel 260 mg/m² intravenously on day 1 every two weeks for four cycles; the control arm received docetaxel 100 mg/m² on the identical schedule. Both cohorts subsequently received epirubicin 90 mg/m² plus cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m² intravenously on day 1 every two weeks for four cycles. Primary endpoints were RCB 0/Ⅰ rate; secondary endpoints encompassed disease free survival (DFS), overall survival (OS), and safety.
Between December 2019 and December 2020, 388 patients (194 per arm) were enrolled. The nab-paclitaxel arm attained an RCB 0/Ⅰ rate of 20.1% (95% CI 14.41-26.72), significantly exceeding the docetaxel arm's 11.34% (95% CI 6.92-15.81; χ²=5.62, P = 0.018). pCR was achieved in 15.98% (95% CI 10.81-21.16) of the nab-paclitaxel group versus 10.31% (95% CI 6.01-14.60) of the docetaxel group (χ²=2.73, P = 0.098). Multivariate analysis confirmed a statistically significant favourable impact of nab-paclitaxel on RCB 0/Ⅰ. Five-year DFS was comparable (90.21% vs. 87.11%; HR: 1.14, 95% CI 0.64-2.05; P = 0.656), as was five-year OS (96.34% vs. 95.31%; HR: 0.64, 95% CI 0.29-1.42; P = 0.274), indicating no significant survival disparity. Grade 3/4 adverse event incidence was similar (30% vs. 38%). The most frequent all-grade toxicities (neutropenia, nausea, myalgia) occurred at comparable rates; however, grade 3/4 peripheral sensory neuropathy was observed exclusively in the nab-paclitaxel cohort. No treatment-related mortality was reported.
A bi-weekly dose-dense nab-paclitaxel regimen followed by dose-dense epirubicin-cyclophosphamide constitutes a feasible and efficacious neoadjuvant strategy for early stage HR+/HER2- breast cancer, delivering a higher RCB 0/Ⅰ rate without compromising overall safety. The increased incidence of high-grade peripheral neuropathy mandates vigilant monitoring, yet the regimen represents a valuable addition to contemporary neoadjuvant options.
