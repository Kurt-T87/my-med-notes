## 📄 Abstract
Accurate pretreatment prediction of pathological complete response (pCR) after neoadjuvant therapy may help guide individualized decision-making in breast cancer. This study aimed to develop and internally validate a practical and interpretable pCR prediction model based on routinely available clinicopathological indicators and MRI-report features, and to explore potential pCR-related biological pathways using a public transcriptomic dataset.
This was a single-center retrospective study, including 201 patients with breast cancer who received neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgery at the General Hospital of Northern Theater Command from April 2021 to April 2025. Patients were divided into pCR and non-pCR groups according to postoperative pathology. Pretreatment clinicopathological and MRI-report features were collected. Univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to identify pCR-related factors, and a clinicopathological model, an MRI-report model, and a combined clinicopathological-MRI model were constructed. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, the DeLong test, calibration curves, the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, decision curve analysis (DCA), and 1000-times bootstrap internal validation. A nomogram was then constructed based on the combined model, and exploratory subgroup applicability was assessed using the single-center cohort. In addition, Hallmark gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using the available public transcriptome data, serving as a preliminary exploration of the biological functional context associated with pCR in breast cancer, and providing an exploratory biological complement to the predictive model.
Among the 201 patients, 60 achieved pCR and 141 had non-pCR. Multivariable logistic regression showed that HER2-positive status, Ki-67 index, and MRI maximum tumor diameter were associated with pCR. The AUC values of the clinicopathological model, MRI-report model, and combined model were 0.880, 0.703, and 0.910, respectively. Although the combined model showed improved AUC compared with the clinicopathological and MRI-report models, the incremental improvement over the clinicopathological model was limited; therefore, its clinical benefit still requires further confirmation by external validation. Calibration analysis, DCA, and bootstrap internal validation suggested good internal stability of the combined model. The nomogram showed favorable performance within the single-center cohort. Exploratory GSEA suggested that differences between pCR and non-pCR may involve TGF-beta signaling, TNF-alpha/NF-kappaB signaling, estrogen response, KRAS-related pathways, and coagulation-related pathways, with heterogeneity across HR/HER2 subgroups.
A clinicopathological-MRI combined model incorporating HER2 status, Ki-67 index, and MRI maximum tumor diameter, together with nomogram construction, may provide a simple and interpretable research tool for predicting pCR after neoadjuvant therapy in breast cancer.
