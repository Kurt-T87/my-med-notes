## 📄 Abstract
A series of 11 compounds of phenyl-imidazole-pyrrole and 9 compounds of phenanthryl-imidazole-pyrrole were synthesized and evaluated against breast cancer cell lines differing in the expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR) and human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER)-2, viz., BT-474 (ER+, PR+, and HER2+), T-47D and MCF-7 (ER+, PR+, and HER2-), and MDA-MB-468 and MDA-MB-231 (ER-, PR-, and HER2-). The analysis of the results indicated that the most active compounds were <b>5d</b> and <b>5k</b> from the phenyl-imidazole-pyrrole series and <b>6b</b> and <b>6i</b> from the phenanthryl-imidazole-pyrrole series (IC<sub>50</sub> < 50 µM). Molecular docking (MD) and molecular dynamics simulation (MDS) studies demonstrated that the active compounds exhibited high binding affinity to the 7NH5 (a co-crystal structure of Akt1 and its specific inhibitor) protein, which is overexpressed in many breast cancer types. The active compounds <b>6b</b> and <b>6i</b> reduced the viability of MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 cells by inducing cell death mechanisms. Both compounds halted the proliferation by arresting cells in the G0/G1 phase of the cell cycle. The results are similar to those of traditional Cisplatin. Intraperitoneal administration of <b>6i</b> retarded the development of EAC tumors and increased the survival of mice compared with tumor-bearing control and 5-fluorouracil (5FU)-treated positive control mice.
