## 📄 Abstract
Adjuvant cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors (CDK4/6i), including abemaciclib and ribociclib, have improved outcomes in patients with high-risk hormone receptor-positive (HR+)/HER2-negative early breast cancer. However, optimal treatment strategies following recurrence during or after adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition remain undefined, as pivotal trials in advanced disease provide limited evidence for this emerging population.
This narrative review critically examines available clinical and translational evidence regarding relapse after adjuvant CDK4/6i, including resistance mechanisms, endocrine sensitivity, biomarker-directed therapies, CDK4/6i rechallenge, chemotherapy, and antibody-drug conjugates. A pragmatic clinical framework is proposed according to the intervals from CDK4/6i discontinuation and endocrine therapy, while integrating disease characteristics, molecular alterations, and treatment feasibility.
Three clinical scenarios are identified: recurrence during or within 12 months after CDK4/6i discontinuation; recurrence more than 12 months after CDK4/6i cessation but during or shortly after endocrine therapy; and late recurrence following completion of both treatments. These categories facilitate clinical assessment but do not represent validated biological definitions of resistance. Evidence supporting CDK4/6i rechallenge and treatments targeting PIK3CA, AKT1, PTEN, or ESR1 alterations is largely extrapolated from metastatic populations. Similarly, the optimal positioning of chemotherapy and antibody-drug conjugates following adjuvant CDK4/6i exposure remains uncertain. Isolated locoregional recurrence requires a distinct, potentially curative multidisciplinary approach. Longitudinal molecular profiling, including circulating tumor DNA and tissue-based multiomics, may improve understanding of resistance and disease evolution.
No established therapeutic sequence exists for patients relapsing after adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition. Treatment selection should integrate relapse timing, endocrine sensitivity, reassessed tumor phenotype, actionable biomarkers, clinical urgency, prior treatment exposure, and patient preferences. Dedicated prospective registries and randomized trials incorporating longitudinal molecular characterization are urgently needed to develop biologically informed treatment strategies.
