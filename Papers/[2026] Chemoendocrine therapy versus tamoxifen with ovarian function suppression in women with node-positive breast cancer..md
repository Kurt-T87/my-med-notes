## 📄 Abstract
Recent trials show a survival benefit from adding chemotherapy to endocrine therapy in premenopausal women with hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative node-positive breast cancer. However, whether chemotherapy remains necessary with ovarian function suppression (OFS) is unclear.
We retrospectively reviewed patients aged ≤ 50 who underwent surgery (2010-2016) for estrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative, pathologic T1-2N1 breast cancer. Patients were classified by adjuvant treatment received as chemoendocrine therapy (chemotherapy plus tamoxifen with or without OFS; n = 590) or tamoxifen plus OFS (n = 141). Recurrence-free survival (RFS) was compared after 1:1 nearest-neighbor propensity score matching (PSM) overall and separately within age groups.
Compared with tamoxifen plus OFS, the chemoendocrine group had higher proportions of T2 tumors, greater nodal burden, grade 3 tumors, progesterone receptor-negative disease, and high Ki-67. After PSM, 7-year RFS was 93.6% (95% CI, 89.7-97.7) with chemoendocrine therapy and 90.9% (95% CI, 86.1-96.0) with tamoxifen plus OFS (HR, 1.58; 95% CI, 0.68-3.66; p = 0.28). No significant difference was detected among patients aged 40 years or older (HR, 1.36; 95% CI, 0.47-3.92; p = 0.57), whereas RFS was lower with tamoxifen plus OFS among those younger than 40 years (Firth HR, 13.81; 95% CI, 1.63-1801.17; p = 0.01). The treatment-by-age interaction was not significant (p = 0.63).
Overall RFS did not differ significantly after PSM. Although the age-stratified findings remain exploratory, tamoxifen plus OFS may merit further evaluation as a chemotherapy-sparing option in selected patients.
