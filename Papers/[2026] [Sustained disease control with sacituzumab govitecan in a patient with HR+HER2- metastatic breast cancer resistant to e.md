## 📄 Abstract
Management of HR+/HER2- metastatic breast cancer after progression on endocrine therapy and a CDK4/6 inhibitor requires integrated assessment of tumor biology, kinetics of progression, disease distribution, comorbidities, and treatment sequencing. We report the case of a 75-year-old woman with arterial hypertension, hypothyroidism, previous HBV infection, and chronic right upper-limb lymphedema, with HR+/HER2- metastatic breast cancer. At disease recurrence, biopsy documented breast carcinoma with ER 98%, PgR 80%, HER2 0, Ki-67 20%, BRCA1/2 wild type status, and no actionable alterations in PIK3CA/AKT/PTEN. The patient received first-line treatment within a clinical trial with palbociclib combined with endocrine therapy, with progression after approximately 6 months, mainly involving the abdominal-peritoneal compartment. The disease was interpreted as primary endocrine-resistant with early resistance to a CDK4/6 inhibitor. Capecitabine was subsequently administered, achieving stable disease at first assessment and progression after approximately 6 months, followed by weekly paclitaxel, discontinued because of further radiological progression. In March 2024, in the presence of abdominal symptoms, weight loss, and ECOG PS 1, sacituzumab govitecan was started. After the first administration, the patient developed diffuse grade 3 skin rash, managed with temporary interruption, corticosteroid therapy, and rechallenge with dose reduction to 7.5 mg/kg. The patient achieved a relevant clinical and radiological response, particularly in the abdominal-peritoneal disease component, with symptom resolution, recovery to ECOG PS 0, and disease control lasting more than 24 months. This case highlights the role of sacituzumab govitecan in fit elderly patients with pretreated, rapidly endocrine-resistant HR+/HER2- breast cancer and limited benefit from conventional chemotherapy, emphasizing proactive toxicity management to preserve treatment continuity and clinical benefit.
