## 📄 Abstract
Data on the risk factors that increase the risk of brain metastases (BM) in breast cancer is lacking. Mining the prospectively collected data of adjuvant studies for BM at first recurrence may identify a population for prevention strategies.
Data collected from 7 sequential adjuvant studies conducted by the Hellenic Cooperative Oncology Group was queried for recurrence in CNS. Parameters associated with the development of BMs at first recurrence were collected.
5,318 patients were included. After a median follow-up time of 137.0 months (range, 0-334.1), the iBCFS at 120 months was 73.2% (95% CI: 72.0%-74.4%) and OS 78.3% (95% CI: 77.2%-79.4%). 106 patients (2%) developed BM at first recurrence and of those 68 (64.2%) had BM only. In cause-specific Cox models fitted on the entire cohort, triple-negative subtype (HR 3.43, 95% CI: 2.05-5.75) and four or more positive axillary nodes (HR 2.52, 1.47-4.33) were independently associated with BM at first recurrence; AJCC stage III (HR 3.41, 1.56-7.44) and hormone receptor negativity (HR 2.08, 1.37-3.15) remained independently associated in alternative parameterizations. Triple negative breast cancer patients had a higher cumulative incidence of BM (3.86%, 95% CI: 2.74-5.42, at 120 months versus 1.27%, 0.90-1.79, in luminal A/B disease; Gray's test p < 0.0001) with a steep rise in the early years after adjuvant therapy. BM patients had significantly worse survival from first recurrence than patients with extracranial first recurrence (median 8.0 versus 31.2 months; HR 2.66, 95% CI: 2.15-3.29), with HER2-positive patients having significantly longer brain metastasis-specific survival (BMSS) than other subtypes (median 13.0 versus 5.4 months; HR 0.56, 95% CI: 0.36-0.86, p = 0.007).
In this large, prospectively annotated database of early breast cancer (eBC) patients, BM seem to occur rarely at first recurrence but carry a poor prognosis. HER2-positive patients with BM had a significantly better prognosis than other subtypes. These estimates describe an era in which adjuvant trastuzumab was reimbursed only from 2006 and contemporary CNS-active regimens were not available and should be read accordingly.
