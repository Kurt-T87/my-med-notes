## 📄 Abstract
Adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibitors have demonstrated significant disease-free survival (DFS) benefits in high-risk early-stage hormone receptor (HR)-positive /HER2-negative breast cancer. However, real-world outcome data describing patients who would meet contemporary eligibility criteria remain limited. This study evaluates treatment outcomes in patients retrospectively classified as eligible for adjuvant ribociclib treatment.
A retrospective cohort analysis was conducted using clinical data from patients with early-stage HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer. Eligibility for adjuvant ribociclib was assigned using the NATALEE trial-based criteria. Differences in treatment outcomes in those eligible versus ineligible patients are reported.
Among the 2730 patients included, 1,955 (71.6%) were high risk and would have met NATALEE eligibility, including 1,679 (85.9%) with node-positive disease, 602 (30.8%) with cT3-4 tumors, and 686 (35.1%) with grade 3 disease; 775 (28.4%) were ineligible due to lower-risk features. Across the cohort, the 5-year DFS rate was significantly worse in CDK4/6i-eligible patients compared to those deemed ineligible: 78.4% versus 91.5% (HR=2.51; 95% CI: 2.01-3.14; <i>p</i><0.0001). Though median OS was not reached, survival curve separation became evident by 36 months and widened progressively. Multivariable Cox regression identified low/intermediate ER expression (<50%), node-positive disease, larger tumors (cT3-4), and grade 3 histology as independent predictors of worse overall survival. Low/intermediate ER expression was associated with significantly poorer OS (HR 1.85, 95% CI 1.27-2.70; p=0.001), while lower-grade and smaller tumors were associated with improved survival.
A significant proportion of patients in this real-world cohort would have met criteria for adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibition. These patients experienced worse survival outcomes compared to their ineligible counterparts. These findings provide real-world historical outcome data for patients who would now be considered candidates for adjuvant ribociclib and may serve as a benchmark for evaluating outcomes following adoption of adjuvant ribociclib.
