## 📄 Abstract
Disease recurrence after definitive therapy remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality in early-stage breast cancer, with limited options for treatment intensification. Cell surface-targeted (CST) therapies, including antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), radiopharmaceuticals, and immune-based approaches, have demonstrated success in treating malignancies. However, the known CST repertoire in breast cancer is narrow, largely restricted to HER2 and TROP2, highlighting an unmet need for novel targets in treatment-resistant disease.
We profiled CST expression using large-scale gene expression datasets annotated with clinical outcomes, including SCAN-B (n ≈ 10,000), aggregated neoadjuvant chemotherapy cohorts (n ≈ 4,000), and the randomized SWEBCG91RT trial (n = 765). CSTs with therapies in clinical trials were prioritized. RNA-protein correlation was assessed using CPTAC and SCMP datasets. Associations with recurrence-free survival (RFS) and treatment response were analyzed using Cox regression and Fisher's exact test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction.
Of 78 CSTs under active clinical investigation, RNA expression strongly predicted protein expression (median accuracy 80-94%). In ER+ breast cancer treated with endocrine therapy, nine CSTs (including CEACAM5/6, EPCAM, NECTIN4) were associated with significantly worse RFS. Of these, EPCAM and CEACAM5 were also associated with poor outcomes specifically after radiotherapy in the SWEBCG91RT trial. In HER2 + disease, high expression of FOLH1 and TNFRSF10B predicted relapse despite anti-HER2 therapy. In triple-negative breast cancer, six CSTs were associated with residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy.
These analyses identify potential candidates for repositioning CST drugs, and single-cell analysis revealed substantial intratumoral heterogeneity for several targets, informing payload selection strategies. CSTs associated with treatment resistance across breast cancer subtypes represent actionable opportunities for therapeutic repositioning. These findings provide a framework for rational integration of CST therapies in early-stage breast cancer.
