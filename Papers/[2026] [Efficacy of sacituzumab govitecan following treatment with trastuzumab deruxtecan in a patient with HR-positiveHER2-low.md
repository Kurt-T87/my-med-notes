## 📄 Abstract
This clinical case addresses a highly relevant issue in the treatment of hormone-receptor positive/HER2-low metastatic breast cancer: the sequential use of antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs). Recently, the therapeutic arsenal has been enriched with two significant agents: trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), indicated for HR+/HER2-low disease, and sacituzumab govitecan (SG), which is indicated for both HR+/HER2-low and HR+/HER2-null disease. The presented case illustrates a beneficial effect from treatment with SG following progression on T-DXd. In the direct sequence of the two ADCs, without the interposition of chemotherapy, a progression-free survival (PFS) of over 12 months with SG was observed, compared to approximately 4 months with T-DXd. The tolerability profile in our clinical case was favorable in both instances, yet subjective manageability and tolerability appeared to favor SG. This case also emphasizes the treatment of an elderly patient, specifically noting that the patient is 75 years old, highlighting that age alone should not be a barrier to optimal treatment. The biological characterization in this case demonstrates apparent homogeneity in HER2 status (HER2-low present at diagnosis, local recurrence, and distant recurrence) and consistent with the literature we observed that SG is active and effective even in tumors with low HER2 expression levels. Moreover, the shift in therapeutic target from HER2 to Trop-2 may plausibly circumvent the resistance mechanisms employed by the tumor.
